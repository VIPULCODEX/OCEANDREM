Categories follow the Hobday marine-heatwave scale (Watch ≥ 0.5°C, Warning ≥ 1.0°C, Severe ≥ 1.5°C, Extreme ≥ 2.0°C above climatology). Argo-style test locations measure subsurface ocean temperature directly, but they are sparse in space and time. Satellites see the surface (SST, salinity, currents) continuously and everywhere. This pipeline learns the relationship between surface satellite observations and subsurface temperature at several depths, so we can estimate subsurface structure in places and times where no direct measurement was taken.

ℹ What's real here, stated plainly: Every tab on this dashboard — Heatwave Monitor, Spatial Observations, Model Benchmarks, Vertical Profiles, and Depth Scatter — runs on real MOSDAC + CMEMS data. There is no simulated or synthetic data anywhere in this build. Two honest limitations remain: the real feature set covers 4 of the problem statement's 5 required surface inputs (SST, SSS, and surface currents are real; a real surface-wind and SSH/SLA product has not yet been obtained, and neither has any synthetic stand-in — they are simply not modeled), and the "1000 m" depth target is approximated by the deepest native level actually available in the pulled CMEMS extract, approximately 902 m. Full detail in the project README and DATASET.md.

The Pipeline, in Four Steps

  1. Collect — real satellite surface data (temperature, salinity, currents) + a real multi-depth CMEMS reanalysis extract as the subsurface target.
  2. Train — seven independent models learn to map surface patterns to underwater temperature.
  3. Test — check the guess against real locations the models never saw.
  4. Monitor — watch for warming events (heatwaves) as new data comes in.

Real Subsurface Target

A real multi-depth CMEMS reanalysis extract (35 native depth levels, ~0.49–902 m) supplies the subsurface temperature target. Real-data loader: build_real_training_table() in real/real_training.py. An independent real Argo float pull (via argopy) was attempted but not completed — see real/PROVENANCE.md.

Research Basis

Loo et al. 2026 (adaptive spatiotemporal clustering) and Wang et al. 2024 (depth-gradient loss, CGKDN) informed this project's methodology — see DATASET.md and README.md for what was and wasn't carried into the current real-data pipeline. Xie et al. 2022's wind-stress-curl input (Attention U-Net) was considered but not carried forward: it required a real surface-wind product this project doesn't have.
